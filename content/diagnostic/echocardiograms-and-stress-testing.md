## About cardiac diagnostics at SMSG

Cardiac diagnostic tests provide the objective information cardiologists and GPs use to assess heart function, investigate symptoms, and monitor known conditions. The most common tests are echocardiograms (ultrasound of the heart) and stress tests (assessment of the heart under exertion).

At SMSG, these tests are typically arranged after a consultation with your GP or with Dr Jennifer Law, our cardiologist at Excelsia. Depending on the specific test and current arrangements, testing may be done on-site or with a partner cardiac imaging provider. Reception can confirm current arrangements for your specific test.

For general ECGs (electrocardiograms), see [Treatment Room & Procedures](/treatment-room-and-procedures/). Standard ECGs are done on-site during a treatment room appointment.

## What the tests cover

### Echocardiogram

An echocardiogram, often called an "echo," is an ultrasound examination of the heart. It uses sound waves to produce moving images of the heart's chambers, valves and blood flow. It's a non-invasive test with no radiation, performed by a sonographer.

An echocardiogram can assess:

- The pumping function of the heart
- Structure and movement of the heart valves
- Blood flow through the heart
- Structure and thickness of the heart walls
- Fluid around the heart
- Congenital heart abnormalities
- Suspected or known heart failure
- Changes after a heart attack

The test takes 30 to 60 minutes. You lie on your side, and the sonographer moves the ultrasound probe across your chest with a gel to improve image quality. You may be asked to move or breathe in specific ways during the test.

### Exercise stress test

An exercise stress test (also called a stress ECG) records the electrical activity of your heart while you exercise on a treadmill or exercise bike. The test is used to:

- Investigate chest pain or symptoms that occur with exertion
- Assess exercise capacity in patients with known heart conditions
- Screen for coronary artery disease in specific situations
- Monitor known conditions

The test typically takes 30 to 45 minutes including preparation and recovery.

### Stress echocardiogram

A stress echocardiogram combines an echocardiogram with a stress test. Ultrasound images of the heart are taken at rest and immediately after exercise, allowing comparison of heart function under the two conditions. This is a more detailed test than either alone.

### Holter and event monitors

For irregular heart rhythms or intermittent symptoms, your doctor may arrange:

- **Holter monitor.** A portable ECG worn for 24 to 48 hours to record heart rhythm continuously.
- **Event monitor.** A longer-term monitor worn for weeks or activated during symptoms, to catch intermittent rhythm disturbances.

These are typically fitted by our nursing team and returned for analysis after the recording period.

## When these tests are ordered

Common reasons include:

- Assessment of chest pain or discomfort
- Palpitations or awareness of the heart beating irregularly
- Shortness of breath, particularly with exertion
- Investigation of murmur heard during a physical examination
- Assessment before certain surgeries in patients with cardiovascular risk
- Monitoring of known heart conditions (heart failure, valve disease, cardiomyopathy)
- Family history of cardiac conditions requiring assessment
- Follow-up after a cardiac event
- Blood pressure that has affected heart structure

Your GP or cardiologist will discuss which specific tests are appropriate for your situation.

## FAQ

**Do I need a referral?**
Yes. Cardiac diagnostic tests require a referral from a GP or specialist for the Medicare rebate to apply.

**Is an echocardiogram painful?**
No. Echocardiogram is a non-invasive ultrasound test. You feel the pressure of the probe on your chest and the gel used to improve image quality, but no pain.

**How do I prepare for a stress test?**
Wear comfortable clothing and walking shoes. Avoid caffeine for 24 hours before the test. Some medications may need to be paused; your doctor will let you know if this applies to you.

**Can I drive home after an echocardiogram or stress test?**
Yes, in most cases. If you're having a stress test with medication rather than exercise (rare in general practice), specific advice will be given.

**How long do results take?**
Reports are typically available within a few days. Discuss results with the ordering doctor.

**What about an ECG?**
Standard ECGs (single-time recording of heart electrical activity) are done in the treatment room, not through this pathway. See [Treatment Room & Procedures](/treatment-room-and-procedures/).

**Are stress tests risky?**
Stress tests are generally safe. They're conducted with close monitoring and appropriate safety precautions. Your doctor will discuss any specific considerations for your situation.
