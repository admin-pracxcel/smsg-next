## What treatment room and procedures cover

### Wound care and dressings

Wounds that don't heal quickly, wounds requiring specialist dressings, and post-procedural wound care are managed in the treatment room. Common reasons include:

- Post-surgical wound care and suture removal
- Chronic wounds (venous leg ulcers, pressure areas, diabetic foot wounds)
- Traumatic wounds requiring cleaning and dressing
- Burns requiring initial or ongoing management
- Complex wounds referred back from hospital

Our nursing team works alongside your GP on wound assessment, dressing selection, and progress monitoring. For wounds needing specialist input, referral pathways are coordinated by your GP.

### Iron infusion

Iron infusion delivers iron directly into the bloodstream and is used when oral iron isn't tolerated, isn't working quickly enough, or when iron deficiency is severe. Common reasons for iron infusion include:

- Iron deficiency anaemia not responding to oral supplements
- Poor tolerance of oral iron
- Chronic disease affecting iron absorption
- Preparation for surgery where iron levels need to improve quickly
- Pregnancy-related iron deficiency where oral supplements haven't been enough
- Post-hospital or post-surgical anaemia

The infusion is given intravenously over about 15 to 30 minutes, followed by a monitoring period. Your GP determines whether iron infusion is appropriate based on your specific iron studies, symptoms, and history.

### ECG and spirometry

**ECG (electrocardiogram).** Records the electrical activity of your heart. Used for assessing palpitations, chest pain, before starting certain medications, before surgery, and as part of cardiovascular assessment. The test takes a few minutes and is painless.

**Spirometry.** Measures how well your lungs are working. Used for diagnosing and monitoring asthma, COPD, and other lung conditions, and as part of pre-employment or driver's licence medicals. The test involves breathing into a mouthpiece under specific instructions.

Both tests are performed on-site during a treatment room appointment.

### Audiology and hearing tests

Hearing assessments are available for adults and children who have concerns about their hearing, or who need a hearing test as part of another assessment (occupational, developmental, or general health).

Audiology tests screen for hearing loss and identify whether further specialist assessment is warranted. Where more detailed testing or hearing aid consideration is needed, referral to an audiologist or ENT specialist can be arranged.

### Ear syringing and ear care

Ear syringing (also called ear irrigation) removes wax that's causing symptoms (hearing loss, discomfort, tinnitus) and can't be managed with drops alone. In modern general practice, ear syringing is done with sterile water or saline using a specific device.

Not everyone with wax needs syringing. Your GP assesses whether the wax is contributing to your symptoms and whether a course of ear drops (to soften the wax) is the right first step. Ear syringing is not appropriate for everyone, including some patients with previous ear surgery, perforations, or specific ear conditions.

### Cryotherapy

Cryotherapy uses liquid nitrogen to treat certain skin lesions by freezing them. Common uses include:

- Warts (viral warts, plantar warts)
- Skin tags
- Actinic keratoses (sun-damaged skin that has precancerous changes)
- Some superficial skin cancers where cryotherapy is the appropriate treatment

The treatment involves applying liquid nitrogen with a spray or cotton bud. It stings briefly and may cause temporary redness, blistering or scab formation. Multiple sessions may be needed for some lesions.

## Fees and billing

Treatment room services are typically billed under Medicare items for the specific procedure. Iron infusion, ECG, spirometry, ear syringing and cryotherapy all have specific Medicare item numbers. Wound care is billed according to the length and complexity of the appointment.

Reception confirms specific fee arrangements at booking.

## FAQ

**Do I need a referral for treatment room services?**
No. Treatment room services are part of GP care.

**How do I book an iron infusion?**
Iron infusion requires a GP consultation first, to review your iron studies and confirm it's appropriate. The infusion is then scheduled as a separate appointment.

**How long does an iron infusion take?**
The infusion itself takes 15 to 30 minutes. With the monitoring period afterwards, plan for about an hour.

**Do you do child immunisations in the treatment room?**
Child immunisations are covered under Travel Medicine and Vaccinations, and involve booking with a GP.

**Do you do PAP smears or cervical screening in the treatment room?**
Cervical screening is done during a GP consultation, generally with an Aurora GP.

**Can I book cryotherapy for warts as a first appointment?**
Book a GP consultation first, so your GP can assess whether cryotherapy is the right treatment for the specific lesion. Cryotherapy may be done in the same appointment or scheduled separately.
