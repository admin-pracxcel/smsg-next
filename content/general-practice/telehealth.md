## About telehealth at SMSG

Telehealth means a consultation done remotely, either by video call or phone. It became a major part of general practice during COVID-19 and has continued as a standard mode of care where it suits.

For SMSG patients, telehealth appointments are booked the same way as in-person appointments. Your GP calls or video-calls you at the appointment time. The consultation is Medicare-rebatable under standard rules (see below).

Telehealth doesn't replace in-person care where a physical examination is needed. Your GP will let you know if an in-person appointment is more appropriate for what you're seeing us about.

---

## When telehealth suits

Common reasons telehealth works well:

- Follow-up appointments where a review is needed but examination isn't
- Mental health consultations
- Discussing test results
- Prescription renewals that need a consultation (see [Scripts & Referrals](/patient-information/scripts-and-referrals/) for automated script requests)
- Specialist referral discussions
- Managing chronic conditions with regular check-ins
- Some new consultations for specific concerns that don't need examination
- Advice on non-urgent symptoms
- Care coordination discussions

---

## When telehealth doesn't suit

Telehealth is not the right approach for:

- New physical symptoms that need examination (chest pain, abdominal pain, rashes, joint pain, injuries)
- Acute illness where a physical exam guides management
- Skin checks and dermatological concerns
- Vaccination appointments
- Any procedure (ECG, spirometry, iron infusion, cryotherapy, dressings)
- Emergencies
- Some new patient consultations where a full physical review is appropriate

Your GP will recommend an in-person appointment if telehealth isn't right for what you're presenting with. Reception can also help you decide at booking.

---

## Medicare rules for telehealth

Under current Medicare rules, telehealth appointments are rebatable when there's an established doctor-patient relationship. This generally means you've had a face-to-face consultation with the same practice within the previous 12 months.

Exceptions to the established relationship rule include:

- Some mental health telehealth items
- Blood-borne virus and reproductive health telehealth items
- Patients in eligible rural and remote areas
- Patients experiencing homelessness
- Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander patients through eligible services

If you haven't seen SMSG in person recently and don't fit an exception, reception will ask you to book an in-person consultation first. This is a Medicare requirement, not an SMSG preference.

## How the appointment works

Book online through Automed and select a telehealth appointment type, or call reception. Confirm your contact number and whether you want a phone or video appointment.

At the appointment time, your GP calls you at the number you've provided. If you're doing a video consultation, you'll receive a link to join a secure video platform shortly before the appointment.

During the consultation:

- Your GP conducts the consultation as they would in person
- Any prescriptions can be sent electronically to your pharmacy
- Any referrals can be prepared and sent
- Any pathology or imaging requests can be issued
- A follow-up plan is agreed

If during the telehealth consultation your GP identifies something that needs in-person review, they'll book that or advise you to attend.

---

## Preparation

Before your telehealth appointment:

- Find a quiet, private space
- Charge your phone or device
- Make sure you have decent phone reception or internet connection
- Have your notes ready (what you want to raise, your medications, any relevant test results)
- If using video, position your camera so your face is well lit and clearly visible
- Have your Medicare card handy

For video appointments, your GP may ask you to use your camera to show a specific area (a skin lesion, an eye, or similar). This is not a substitute for physical examination but can help.

## Where it's offered

## Fees and billing

Telehealth appointments follow the same billing arrangements as in-person appointments. Medicare rebates apply where the appointment meets Medicare's telehealth eligibility rules.

Bulk-billing arrangements for telehealth vary by practitioner. Reception confirms specific fee arrangements at booking.

## FAQ

**Do I need a referral for telehealth?**
No. Telehealth is a mode of consultation, not a separate service. If you're seeing your usual GP, it's booked directly.

**Can new patients book telehealth?**
Under Medicare rules, an established relationship is generally required for the Medicare rebate. New patients typically need an in-person consultation first, unless they qualify for one of the exceptions.

**Can specialists at Excelsia offer telehealth?**
Yes. Some specialists offer telehealth appointments for eligible patients. Contact reception to ask about a specific specialist.

**Is video better than phone?**
For most consultations, either works. Video can help where visual assessment adds something (a skin lesion, a wound, an eye concern). Phone is simpler and doesn't require good internet or a camera.

**Is telehealth secure and private?**
Yes. The platforms used are compliant with Australian privacy standards for healthcare. Your consultation notes are kept in your file as they would be for an in-person consultation.

**What if the connection drops out during a video call?**
Your GP will try to reconnect. If the connection is unstable, the appointment usually continues by phone.

**Can telehealth prescriptions be sent to my pharmacy?**
Yes. Electronic prescriptions are sent to any Australian pharmacy of your choice.
