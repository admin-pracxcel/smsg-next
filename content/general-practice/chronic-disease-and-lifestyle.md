## What chronic disease and lifestyle care covers

### Chronic Disease Management Plans

Chronic Disease Management (CDM) Plans, also called GP Management Plans, are Medicare-funded structured plans for patients with a chronic health condition. They provide a written framework for managing your condition, coordinating care between your GP and other providers, and reviewing progress at set intervals.

CDM Plans open access to bulk-billed allied health sessions (dietitian, physiotherapist, psychologist, podiatrist, and others) as part of the plan. This is particularly valuable for patients whose condition benefits from coordinated multidisciplinary care.

Conditions commonly managed with a CDM Plan include diabetes, asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), heart disease, chronic kidney disease, mental health conditions, arthritis, and other long-term illnesses. Your GP will discuss whether a CDM Plan is appropriate for your situation.

### Smoking cessation

Smoking cessation is one of the highest-value health interventions available. Your GP can support you with:

- Assessment of your current smoking and readiness to change
- Nicotine replacement therapy (patches, gum, lozenges, inhalers)
- Prescription medications that support quitting
- Behavioural strategies and referral to counselling or Quitline
- Follow-up appointments through the quit process

Quitting isn't a single decision; it's often a series of attempts. Support at each attempt substantially improves the chance of long-term success.

### Diabetes management

Diabetes management is coordinated between your GP and, where relevant, an endocrinologist, dietitian, diabetes educator, and other allied health. Your GP is typically the coordinator.

Care includes regular monitoring of glucose, cholesterol, kidney function, eyes, feet, and cardiovascular risk. Medications are adjusted based on how you're going. For patients with Type 1 or complex Type 2 diabetes, endocrinologist input at Excelsia is available.

Prevention and reversal of Type 2 diabetes and pre-diabetes is a growing area. Weight management, dietary change, exercise and, where appropriate, medication can substantially reduce risk of progression.

### Asthma and COPD

Asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) both involve airway inflammation and are managed with a combination of medication, avoiding triggers, and monitoring lung function. Your GP:

- Confirms the diagnosis with spirometry where relevant
- Establishes an appropriate medication plan
- Prepares an Asthma Action Plan or COPD Action Plan
- Reviews control at set intervals
- Adjusts treatment as your condition evolves

For asthma or COPD that is difficult to control, respiratory specialist input at Excelsia is available.

### Medications review

Reviewing your medications regularly is part of good care. Multiple medications can interact, some may no longer be needed, and some may benefit from a different dose or timing. Your GP can review your medications during a routine appointment, or a dedicated Home Medicines Review (HMR) can be arranged for patients on multiple medications.

Home Medicines Reviews involve an accredited pharmacist visiting you at home to conduct a full review, followed by a written report to your GP. The GP then discusses the findings with you and makes any changes to your medication plan. This is Medicare-funded for eligible patients.

## Fees and billing

Chronic Disease Management Plans, care plan reviews, and Home Medicines Reviews are Medicare-funded services, and are typically bulk-billed for eligible patients.

Standard consultations for chronic disease management follow the same billing arrangements as other consultations. Longer appointments (for care plan preparation, complex reviews, or comprehensive assessment) may attract a private fee with a Medicare rebate applying.

Bulk-billed allied health sessions accessed through a CDM Plan are Medicare-funded up to the annual entitlement (currently five sessions per calendar year across all allied health under a CDM Plan).

Reception confirms specific fee arrangements at booking.

## FAQ

**Do I need a referral for a Chronic Disease Management Plan?**
No. Plans are prepared by your GP directly. You need to have an eligible condition and typically an established relationship with the practice.

**How often is the plan reviewed?**
Plans are reviewed at defined intervals, typically every 3 to 6 months, depending on your condition.

**Can I access allied health without a CDM Plan?**
Yes. You can see allied health practitioners privately at any time. A CDM Plan opens access to bulk-billed sessions under Medicare.

**What's the difference between a CDM Plan and a Team Care Arrangement?**
A GP Management Plan (part of a CDM Plan) is prepared by your GP. A Team Care Arrangement is prepared when other providers are involved in your care. Both are usually done together for patients accessing bulk-billed allied health.

**Can I get help to quit smoking without a CDM Plan?**
Yes. Smoking cessation support can be provided in a standard consultation with your GP, and Quitline is a free national resource on 13 78 48.

**Who prepares a Home Medicines Review?**
Your GP refers to an accredited pharmacist. The pharmacist visits you at home and prepares a report for your GP to act on.
