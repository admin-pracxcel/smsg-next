## What preventive care covers

### Health assessments

Structured health assessments look at your risk profile across body systems and lifestyle factors. Medicare funds specific assessments at particular life stages.

**45 to 49 lifestyle risk assessment.** For adults aged 45 to 49 who have a chronic disease risk factor. Covers cardiovascular risk, diabetes risk, cancer screening, lifestyle factors, and mental health. Designed to identify preventable conditions before they develop.

**75 and older health assessment.** For older adults, this is a comprehensive review of physical function, cognition, medications, mental health, social circumstances, and preventive care. Can be conducted at your home if that's easier.

**715 Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander health check.** Available annually for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people of any age. Comprehensive review tailored to your stage of life. This check also opens access to bulk-billed follow-up services through Chronic Disease Management pathways.

**General health checks.** Available at other ages where your GP identifies a specific reason (family history, symptoms, occupational risk, or a request from you).

### Bowel cancer screening

The National Bowel Cancer Screening Program mails free at-home test kits to Australians aged 50 to 74 every two years. The test is a simple faecal occult blood test done at home and returned by mail.

Your SMSG GP receives your result and contacts you if follow-up is needed. If the test is positive, this doesn't mean you have cancer; it means further investigation is warranted, usually with a colonoscopy referral. Your GP will discuss the next step.

Bowel cancer is one of the most preventable cancers when detected early. The five-year survival rate for bowel cancer detected at stage 1 is over 99 per cent.

If you're outside the 50 to 74 age range but have a family history of bowel cancer, discuss earlier or more frequent screening with your GP.

### Men's health checks

Men's health checks are structured consultations focused on the health concerns that affect men across different life stages: cardiovascular risk, diabetes risk, mental health, prostate concerns, sexual health, sleep, weight and lifestyle. Some concerns come up more readily when there's a dedicated appointment to raise them.

At SMSG, men's health checks can be booked at any time, and don't require a referral. If you have a specific concern (blood pressure, cholesterol, a lump you've noticed, energy or mood changes, sleep), book it. If you're due for a general review, book it.

### Women's health checks

Women's health checks cover a wide range across the life course: cervical screening, breast health, contraception, fertility, menopause, cardiovascular risk, mental health, bone health and preventive care. Some checks are opportunistic (during an appointment about something else); others are dedicated appointments.

For women looking for specific women's health focus, Aurora Women & Babies Health at SMSG offers dedicated women's health care. Aurora is described on its own page.

## Fees and billing

Preventive care appointments are billed according to the length and complexity of the consultation. Some Medicare-funded health assessments (45 to 49 lifestyle risk, 75+ health assessment, 715 Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander health check) are bulk-billed for eligible patients. Bowel cancer screening kits from the national program are free.

Longer appointments and complex reviews may attract a private fee with a Medicare rebate applying. Reception confirms specific fee arrangements at booking.

## FAQ

**Do I need a referral for a preventive check?**
No. Preventive care with your GP doesn't require a referral.

**Am I eligible for the 45 to 49 health assessment?**
The assessment is for adults aged 45 to 49 with at least one chronic disease risk factor. Common risk factors include family history of chronic disease, lifestyle factors, and existing conditions. Ask reception or your GP to check eligibility.

**How do I get a bowel cancer screening kit?**
Kits are mailed to eligible Australians automatically. If yours hasn't arrived or you've lost it, you can request a replacement through the National Bowel Cancer Screening Program. Your GP can also help.

**What if I'm outside the standard screening age?**
Discuss with your GP. Age-based screening programs are population defaults. Individual risk (family history, previous polyps, symptoms) may warrant different arrangements.

**Can I have a general check-up if I'm not due for a specific assessment?**
Yes. Book with your GP and describe what you're looking for. A general review can be structured around your specific concerns.
