## About the service

Antenatal shared care is the model Australian public hospitals offer for women whose pregnancies are progressing normally. Rather than travelling to the hospital for every routine check, you see your GP for most visits and the hospital for the milestone appointments, ultrasounds and the birth.

For women in Sydney's Inner West and south, the local partner hospitals are the Royal Hospital for Women, St George Hospital and Sutherland Hospital. Each has its own shared care program with specific gestational windows for hospital visits and a schedule of GP visits in between. Aurora GPs work within the guidelines of each program so that whichever hospital you're booked at, your care aligns with what the hospital expects at each stage.

The Aurora team at SMSG has done shared care as long as SMSG has existed. Several of our GPs are on the accredited shared care lists at the local hospitals. If you already know which hospital you'll give birth at, we'll match you to a GP who works with that program.

## What shared care looks like

Once your pregnancy is confirmed and you've had your first Aurora consultation, we help you register with the hospital shared care program. From there, the structure is roughly this.

**Weeks 6 to 12.** Confirmation of pregnancy, dating scan referral, first bloods and screening tests, discussion of your options for care. This is where we work out whether shared care suits your situation.

**Weeks 12 to 20.** First trimester screening results reviewed, second trimester bloods, morphology scan referral at 18 to 20 weeks. Alternating GP and hospital visits begin.

**Weeks 20 to 36.** Regular antenatal visits with your Aurora GP, hospital visits at the intervals set by your specific program. Your GP checks blood pressure, urine, foetal growth, position and movement, and talks through anything that comes up.

**Weeks 36 onwards.** Hand-over to the hospital for delivery. Your GP remains available for postnatal review, six-week check-up, contraception advice, immunisations for baby, and support with breastfeeding.

Every hospital program is slightly different. Your Aurora GP will explain the schedule for your specific program at your first visit.

## When shared care isn't the right fit

Shared care suits pregnancies that are progressing normally with no significant medical or obstetric concerns. If your history or your current pregnancy carries higher-than-average risk, you may need midwifery-led care or full obstetric care at the hospital instead.

Reasons your GP might discuss a different model include a previous high-risk pregnancy, a chronic health condition affecting the pregnancy, a multiple pregnancy, a pregnancy conceived through assisted reproduction where the specialist team wants continuity, or preferences you have around the birth.

At Aurora, we also offer full obstetric care through Dr Aye Thidar Htun, our Obstetrician & Gynaecologist. If shared care is not the fit for you, we can discuss whether Aurora obstetric care works better.

## Fees and billing

For eligible patients, most antenatal shared care consultations are bulk-billed. Longer consultations for pregnancy planning, birth preparation and complex reviews may attract a private fee with a Medicare rebate applying.

Reception confirms the specific arrangement with your chosen GP when you book.

## FAQ

**Do I need a referral to start shared care with Aurora?**
No. Book directly with any Aurora GP.

**Can I keep my usual GP outside SMSG for general care?**
Yes. Many women see their existing GP for non-pregnancy issues and use Aurora for their pregnancy care. We coordinate with your GP so nothing falls between the cracks.

**Which hospitals do Aurora GPs share care with?**
Aurora GPs work with the shared care programs at the Royal Hospital for Women, St George Hospital and Sutherland Hospital. If you're planning to birth at a private hospital, we can discuss what care model works.

**What happens after the birth?**
Your Aurora GP is available for postnatal review, six-week check, immunisations, contraception advice and support with breastfeeding. Dr Yashodha Ediriweera has IBCLC lactation training if you want additional lactation support.

**Can I combine shared care with an Aurora obstetrician?**
Yes. Dr Aye Thidar Htun sees Aurora patients where the shared care model isn't right, or where you'd prefer a single consultant through pregnancy. Talk to your Aurora GP about the options at your first visit.
