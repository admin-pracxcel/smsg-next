## About the test

The Cervical Screening Test replaced the two-yearly Pap smear in December 2017. It tests directly for the human papillomavirus (HPV), the virus responsible for almost all cervical cancers. Because HPV testing is more accurate than Pap smear cytology, the test only needs to be done every five years for most women rather than every two.

The National Cervical Screening Program recommends a Cervical Screening Test every five years for anyone with a cervix aged 25 to 74 who has ever been sexually active. This includes women who have received the HPV vaccine, women no longer sexually active, and women in same-sex relationships. The vaccine reduces but does not eliminate HPV risk, and screening remains part of standard preventive care.

If your last Pap smear or Cervical Screening Test was negative, your next test is due five years from that date. If you're not sure when yours is due, reception can check for you or contact your previous provider.

## Clinician-collected or self-collected

Since July 2022, all women eligible for cervical screening can choose between a clinician-collected sample and a self-collected sample. Both tests are equally accurate for detecting HPV.

**Clinician-collected.** The doctor takes a sample from the cervix using a speculum, in the same way as a traditional Pap smear. This takes a few minutes and is completed in the consultation.

**Self-collected.** You take the sample yourself, in a private space at the centre, using a swab that comes as a simple kit. You insert the swab a short distance into the vagina and rotate it a few times, then return the swab to the reception. No speculum needed.

Both samples go to the same laboratory. Results come back to your GP in around 5 to 10 business days.

Self-collection has increased screening participation among women who had previously avoided or delayed the test. If you've been hesitant to book because you find the traditional test uncomfortable or difficult, self-collection is a legitimate option and the outcome is clinically equivalent.

## When you'll be recalled

If your test is negative for HPV, your next screening is in five years. We add a reminder to your file and contact you as it approaches, but you don't need to think about it in the interim.

If your test detects HPV but not the higher-risk types associated with cancer (HPV types other than 16 and 18), we recall you in twelve months for a repeat test to see whether your immune system has cleared the virus.

If your test detects HPV types 16 or 18, or if repeat testing shows the virus persisting, we refer you for a colposcopy at a specialist centre. Colposcopy is a more detailed examination of the cervix and is a standard next step, not a diagnosis. Most colposcopies do not find cancer.

## Fees and billing

Cervical screening consultations are bulk-billed for eligible patients. If you're combining the appointment with a broader women's health check or other concerns, a longer consultation may attract a private fee with a Medicare rebate applying.

The Cervical Screening Test itself is Medicare-funded. There is no separate laboratory fee for eligible patients.

## FAQ

**Do I need a referral?**
No. Book directly with any Aurora GP.

**What if I've had the HPV vaccine?**
You still need cervical screening. The vaccine reduces risk of the HPV types that most commonly cause cancer, but it doesn't cover all types, and screening remains the standard of care.

**Can I have the test if I'm pregnant?**
Cervical screening can be done during pregnancy but is often deferred to the postnatal period unless there's a specific concern. Talk to your GP about timing.

**Can I have the test during my period?**
Yes, though heavy bleeding can affect sample quality. Light bleeding is usually fine. If you're on the heaviest days of your period, we may suggest rebooking.

**What about women who have had a hysterectomy?**
This depends on the reason for the hysterectomy and whether your cervix was removed. Your GP will confirm whether ongoing screening is needed for you.

**What about women over 74?**
The formal program ends at 74, but if you've never been screened or your most recent test was more than five years ago, screening may still be appropriate. Talk to your GP.

**What if the test is abnormal?**
Most "abnormal" results reflect an HPV finding rather than cancer. Your GP will explain your specific result and the next step, which is usually a repeat test in twelve months or a referral for colposcopy.
