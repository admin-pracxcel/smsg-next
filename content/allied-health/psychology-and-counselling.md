## Psychology vs counselling

The distinction between psychology and counselling can be confusing. Broadly:

**Psychologists** are university-trained in psychological assessment and therapy. They are registered with AHPRA under the Psychology Board of Australia. Psychologists provide therapy for a wide range of mental health conditions and psychological concerns.

**Clinical psychologists** hold additional postgraduate training in the assessment and treatment of complex mental health conditions. Their sessions attract a slightly higher Medicare rebate under a Mental Health Care Plan.

**Counsellors and psychotherapists** are trained in therapy modalities and work with adults, adolescents and families across life concerns, relationship difficulties, grief, and adjustment. Counsellors are not registered with AHPRA but hold professional membership (for example, PACFA or ACA).

In practice, the differences can be less important than finding the right practitioner for your situation. Reception can help you decide who to book with.

## Mental Health Care Plans

For Medicare-rebated psychology and counselling sessions, a Mental Health Care Plan is prepared by your GP. Under a Mental Health Care Plan, you can access up to ten Medicare-rebated sessions per calendar year, split into an initial six sessions with a GP review before accessing the final four.

Not every situation calls for a Mental Health Care Plan. Some patients prefer to see a psychologist or counsellor privately, without a formal plan. Others find the plan process useful. Discuss with reception or your GP what fits.

## Fees and billing

Psychology and counselling sessions are privately billed. Under a Mental Health Care Plan, Medicare rebates apply for eligible sessions (higher rebate for clinical psychology). The gap between session fee and Medicare rebate varies by practitioner.

Private health fund rebates apply for patients with psychology or counselling extras cover; check with your health fund.

Reception confirms specific fee arrangements at booking.

## FAQ

**Do I need a referral to see a psychologist or counsellor?**
No. You can book directly. A GP-prepared Mental Health Care Plan is required if you want to access the Medicare rebate.

**How long is a session?**
Sessions are typically 45 to 60 minutes.

**How many sessions will I need?**
That varies substantially by presentation and goals. Some patients need a small number of sessions; others benefit from longer-term therapy. Your practitioner discusses this at your first session.

**Can I choose between a psychologist and a counsellor?**
Yes. If you're not sure, reception can help based on what you're seeing us about. Sometimes practitioners themselves suggest a different colleague after an initial consultation if they think another approach would fit better.

**Are your practitioners LGBTQIA+ affirming?**
Yes.

**Are your practitioners neurodivergence-affirming?**
Sandra Bell specifically describes her practice as neurodiversity-affirming. Other SMSG practitioners work with neurodivergent patients across their practice.

**Can children and adolescents be seen?**
Yes. Sandra Bell, Sue Boursiani and Cara Chillari work with children and adolescents through Kids' Dr.

**Is there a wait for appointments?**
Wait times vary by practitioner. Reception can advise on current availability.
