## About dietetics at SMSG

Accredited Practising Dietitians (APDs) are qualified to translate nutrition evidence into practical dietary changes for individuals. They work with the range of conditions where diet plays a role: chronic disease, weight management, gastrointestinal conditions, allergy and intolerance, feeding difficulties, and paediatric growth and nutrition.

## What dietetics covers

**Chronic disease.** Diabetes, cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, and other conditions where diet is a core part of management. Often accessed under a Chronic Disease Management Plan.

**Weight management.** Structured support for sustainable weight change. Dietitians help you plan and adapt what you eat in a way that fits your life.

**Gastrointestinal conditions.** Irritable bowel syndrome, inflammatory bowel disease, reflux, coeliac disease and other conditions where dietary triggers or nutritional deficiency need addressing.

**Food allergy and intolerance.** Structured elimination and reintroduction approaches for suspected food intolerances, and management of confirmed allergies.

**Paediatric nutrition (through Kids' Dr).** Feeding difficulties, growth concerns, fussy eating, nutritional deficiencies, and dietary support for children with developmental or medical conditions.

**Pregnancy and postnatal nutrition.** For patients receiving pregnancy care through Aurora, dietetic input on gestational diabetes, iron and folate, and postnatal nutrition.

**Older adult nutrition.** Nutrition changes with age, unintended weight loss, muscle mass concerns, and dietary support alongside geriatric assessment.

**Eating concerns and disordered eating.** For patients with disordered eating patterns, dietetics is part of a coordinated team approach alongside psychology and medical review.

## What happens in an appointment

The first appointment involves:

- A detailed history of your current diet, medical history, medications, activity and lifestyle
- Assessment of your goals and any specific concerns
- Discussion of practical changes that could fit your situation
- A plan for follow-up sessions where needed
- Coordination with your GP or paediatrician where relevant

Dietetics generally involves more than one session. Behaviour change takes time, and follow-up appointments help you refine what's working and adjust what isn't.

## FAQ

**Do I need a referral?**
No. Dietetics can be booked directly. A GP referral under a Chronic Disease Management Plan is required to access the Medicare rebate.

**How long is a session?**
Initial appointments are typically 45 to 60 minutes. Follow-up appointments are usually 30 to 45 minutes.

**How many sessions will I need?**
That varies substantially by concern. Your dietitian discusses expected timeframes at the first session.

**Do dietitians provide meal plans?**
Sometimes. Meal plans work for some patients and not others. Dietitians typically focus on strategies you can adapt over time rather than fixed plans that may not fit your life beyond the short term.

**Are dietitians the same as nutritionists?**
No. In Australia, "dietitian" (specifically Accredited Practising Dietitian) is a regulated title requiring specific qualifications. "Nutritionist" is not regulated and covers a wider range of training. Both of our dietitians are Accredited Practising Dietitians.

**Can dietitians work with eating disorders?**
Yes. Dietetics for eating disorders is a specialised area and is coordinated with psychology and medical review. Discuss with reception whether the dietitians on our team are the right fit for your specific presentation.

**Can I use my NDIS plan?**
Yes, where dietetics is included in your plan. Discuss at booking.
