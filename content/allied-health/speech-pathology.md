## About speech pathology at SMSG

Speech pathologists work with communication and swallowing across the life course. They assess and treat concerns with speech (how words are produced), language (how words are understood and used), voice, fluency (stuttering), and swallowing.

**Sarah Impellizzeri.** Speech pathologist consulting through Kids' Dr at Earlwood and Sans Souci.

**TLC Speech Therapy (visiting service).** A visiting speech therapy tenant service that consults from SMSG premises. This is an external service delivered on SMSG premises; bookings are through TLC directly.

---

## What speech pathology covers

**Speech.** How individual sounds are produced. Common concerns include speech sound disorders in children, articulation difficulties, and speech changes following stroke or other neurological events in adults.

**Language.** How language is understood and expressed. Language concerns can affect children whose language milestones are delayed, children with autism or other developmental conditions, and adults with acquired language difficulties.

**Fluency.** Stuttering, cluttering, and other fluency concerns in children and adults.

**Voice.** Hoarseness, voice loss, vocal fatigue, and voice conditions related to specific occupations or activities.

**Swallowing.** Difficulty swallowing (dysphagia), including safety concerns with eating and drinking.

**Feeding (infants).** Feeding difficulties in infants, including breastfeeding, bottle feeding, and transition to solids.

**Literacy.** Reading, writing, spelling and language concerns that affect academic progress.

**Autism and social communication.** Assessment and support for the communication aspects of autism spectrum conditions, delivered within the Kids' Dr multidisciplinary framework.

## When speech pathology suits

For children:

- Delay in reaching speech and language milestones
- Difficulty being understood by people outside the family
- Stuttering
- Difficulty following instructions or understanding language
- Feeding or swallowing concerns
- Concerns related to autism, ADHD or developmental conditions
- Literacy difficulties affecting school progress
- Preparation for school

For adults:

- Voice changes not resolving
- Swallowing difficulties
- Communication changes following stroke, brain injury, or other neurological conditions
- Stuttering
- Communication concerns related to specific occupations (teaching, singing, public speaking)

## What happens in an appointment

The first appointment involves:

- A detailed history from you (and, for children, from a parent)
- Assessment of the specific area of concern
- Discussion of findings and recommendations
- A plan for follow-up sessions where needed
- Coordination with your GP, paediatrician, or other clinicians as relevant

For children being assessed alongside a Kids' Dr paediatrician (for example, as part of an autism assessment), speech pathology forms part of the multidisciplinary picture.

## FAQ

**Do I need a referral?**
No. Speech pathology can be booked directly. A GP referral under a Chronic Disease Management Plan is required to access the Medicare rebate.

**How long is a session?**
Sessions are typically 45 to 60 minutes for assessment and 30 to 45 minutes for follow-up.

**How many sessions will my child need?**
That varies significantly by the concern. Your speech pathologist will discuss expectations at the first session.

**Can Sarah see adults?**
Sarah's primary focus is paediatric through Kids' Dr. Reception can confirm current availability for adult patients.

**What's the difference between Sarah and TLC?**
Sarah is part of the SMSG allied health team, working within Kids' Dr. TLC is an external speech pathology service that rents rooms at SMSG. Both are qualified speech pathology services; the arrangements and fee structures differ.

**Can I use my NDIS plan?**
Yes, where speech pathology is included in your plan. Discuss at booking.

**Is speech pathology different from speech therapy?**
The two terms are used interchangeably in Australia. "Speech pathology" is the term used by the professional body (Speech Pathology Australia); "speech therapy" is commonly used in everyday language.
