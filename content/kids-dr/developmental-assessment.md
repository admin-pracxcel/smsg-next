## About the service

Children develop at different rates, and variation within the typical range is common. Sometimes a difference is a phase. Sometimes it's a sign of something worth understanding better. Developmental assessment is the appointment to explore that question with a paediatrician who takes a broad view of your child.

Common reasons to book:

- Delay in speech milestones
- Late walking or persistent motor coordination differences
- Difficulty in concentration or with studies
- Sensitive emotional outbursts
- Fussy eating behaviour or feeding concerns
- Weight and growth under or over expected patterns
- Balance and posture coordination concerns
- Social differences at preschool or school
- Sleep, toileting or general behaviour patterns you'd like a paediatrician's view on

Kids' Dr paediatricians assess the developmental picture as a whole. Where a specific concern (such as ADHD or autism) becomes the focus, we move into that pathway. Where the picture is broader, the assessment stays broad, and support is coordinated across the multidisciplinary Kids' Dr team where relevant.

## What assessment involves

Developmental assessment considers the whole child. Your paediatrician gathers information from several sources.

**A detailed developmental history.** Your paediatrician walks through your child's development from early years to now, including the current concerns and any allied health input to date.

**Clinical review.** Your paediatrician examines and interacts with your child in the consultation. For younger children, play-based observation forms part of this.

**Input from education and care settings.** Where relevant, information from your child's preschool, school or other setting helps ground the picture.

**Consideration of what else may be contributing.** Developmental patterns can reflect many things: growth, hearing, vision, sleep, nutrition, family circumstances, learning environment, and specific developmental conditions. Assessment considers what's contributing to your child's picture.

Depending on what emerges, your paediatrician may recommend further assessment or targeted allied health input.

## What might follow assessment

**Reassurance and monitoring.** For many families, assessment ends with a paediatrician's view that development is progressing within expected variation. Follow-up appointments may be scheduled to check in over time.

**Targeted allied health.** Where a specific area (speech, motor coordination, feeding, learning) needs support, your paediatrician can coordinate referrals within the Kids' Dr multidisciplinary team. This includes speech pathology, physiotherapy, dietetics, psychology and counselling.

**Further specialist assessment.** Where the assessment suggests a specific condition (autism, ADHD, learning disorder, or another paediatric concern), your paediatrician will discuss the appropriate next step.

**Ongoing paediatric care.** For families with ongoing questions, Kids' Dr paediatricians provide continuity across visits so you don't have to start from the beginning each time.

## FAQ

**Do I need a referral?**
Yes. A GP referral is required for the Medicare rebate to apply.

**Is my concern serious enough to book?**
If you're wondering, it's worth asking. Paediatricians see the full range from mild questions to significant concerns. Early input often helps regardless of the outcome of assessment.

**How is developmental assessment different from ADHD or autism assessment?**
Developmental assessment is broader and can be a starting point. Where a specific condition (like ADHD or autism) emerges as the picture, the appropriate condition-specific pathway begins.

**Will my child need multiple appointments?**
Sometimes. Your paediatrician will discuss what's needed after the first appointment.

**Can Kids' Dr work with my child's school or preschool?**
Yes, with your consent. School and preschool information often adds valuable context.

**What if my child has been assessed before?**
Bring existing reports. Kids' Dr can build on previous work rather than starting from scratch.
